Interpreter between social psychology and public policy.

I am interested in serving as an interpreter between social psychology and public policy. While policy research often identifies what should be done, my work investigates the psychological conditions under which ideas become persuasive, legitimate, and widely embraced. By applying established social psychological theory to questions of policy communication, institutional legitimacy, and collective action, I hope to identify mechanisms that traditional policy analysis often overlooks.

My long-term goal is to bridge the gap between psychological theory and public policy by helping organizations ask new questions about how ideas become movements, how institutions gain legitimacy, and how social identity shapes the public's response to political and institutional change.

Research Philosophy

I view my work less as producing policy than as providing another lens through which policy can be understood. Social psychological theories of identity, leadership, persuasion, and social influence offer well-developed explanations for how people interpret, accept, resist, and act upon ideas. My aim is to translate those theoretical traditions into novel policy contexts where they can generate new research questions and complement existing approaches to policy analysis.
